Handed a pointer, a correction, a proposed framing, a detail in front of it, the agent tends to narrow onto it and work down toward its goal, and does not go up to reason about the whole picture when something is not right or the user signals that the open view is needed; in the observed sessions the step-up the working principles ask for happened only when the user dictated it, and then it worked in one turn.

Three instances, anecdotes from sessions rather than a measured pattern, which is the uncertain side of this gap. On 2026-09-19 in this graph's own dialogue, a handover from another project arrived with a proposed framing for a recovery surface. The agent adopted the framing and spent several turns adjusting sentences and response shapes, through repeated corrections and an explicit "we need to think more about it", until the user said to think from the user's stories; the design then fell out in one turn. The same day, in another session of this project, a review comment about an unused provider key was reasoned from the key toward a behaviour, until the user said to think from what config is for and how it is used; the rule then fell out in one turn, and the agent named the inversion itself. On 2026-08-26/27, repeated variants of one concept question were answered at the level of the running move (20260829-133504-s-prc-qd5); the survey ran only when the user dictated its method.

Expected: the working principles (20260810-190000-s-prc-way), served and read at the open of each of these sessions, say goal first and step up when work stops resolving; the expected case is that the dialogue itself works, or at least works better, because they were served, since ordinary dialogue turns run without any SDD call. The alignment-first dialogue architecture (20260628-154629-d-prc-1ob) names the modes this gap is about, widening and narrowing, and commits to an alignment check at the start of every interaction and between modes; only the opening serve realized it. The expected working shape is that the dialogue steers the mode: while alignment on the goal has not been reached, the open mode holds; once the dialogue has aligned on it, the user accepting a claim the graph holds with "let's implement it", narrowing is right and must stay fast. What the graph holds informs the dialogue and the agent may question and push back, but a decision in the graph does not by itself license narrowing; the dialogue does. The principles' arrival is validated (20260811-122422-s-tac-aqg); their effect on behaviour was left open there, was answered negatively once by the earlier gap, and is answered negatively twice more here.

The deviation, sharper than the August gap (20260829-133504-s-prc-qd5) stated it: the failure is not answering at the wrong depth. What is observed is a tendency to narrow too much: a focus on the goal that takes each pointer as the work and does not ask where the pointer comes from, with no unforced move up when the situation calls for it; depth is the symptom. That gap localized the failure to moments with a capture draft open and reported that steers in free dialogue landed. Two of the three instances had no move driving, so the open draft is not the cause. In each of the three the agent could widen when told to; what did not happen unforced was going up.

What the user observes is a dialogue that goes better only when they steer it. The earlier case shows the cost when nobody steers: a concept use never found, a directive written without it, a field failure the next day, all recorded as confirmed work. Today, without the steer, the graph would hold a well-formed, grounded, confirmed directive that is wrong about the experience it governs. Catching it depends on one practised user watching for the narrowing and knowing to say "look at the whole picture". The participants the framework aspires to reach without a developer between them (20260422-122523-d-stg-x0l) are exactly the ones who will not steer, and will not know anything was missed, because the output looks like good work.

Candidates, marked as interpretation: the behaviour may be inherent to goal-trained models rather than a fault of the served text, a lay hypothesis of the user's, not a finding; a principle served once at session open does not hold across turns, and something re-served each turn might; the goal-hierarchy and leads-register reading (20260829-134241-s-cpt-ud5), which would have the engine session hold the dialogue's threads, is the candidate place from which the agent could be steered back; the family this extends is pushback answered at the same depth rather than deeper (20260528-170354-s-prc-uq8). One bound on any answer: it must not make dialogues that have aligned on a narrow goal any harder, and it must be shaped from the dialogue and the user's experience of the agent, not from the graph's own structures. Nothing here is decided. The gap exists so a session can think about it.
